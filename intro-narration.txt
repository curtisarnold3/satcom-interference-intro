Every unit, satellite, and organization in this story is fictional.

Forward Site Orion. A small deployed team brings its satellite terminal up, far from any network.

That dish is their main line home. Command chat, the voice net, and live video all ride on it.

Terminal A transmits up to Kestrel Seven, thirty-five thousand, seven hundred eighty-six kilometers above the equator. That's the uplink, at fourteen point two hundred fifty gigahertz.

Transponder K-U fourteen gigahertz shifts the signal down twenty-three hundred megahertz and beams it back. That's the downlink, at eleven point nine hundred fifty giga hertz.

Everyone under the beam hears it: the team's headquarters, the ops center, and the SATCOM monitoring station.

Then, somewhere inside the uplink footprint, an unknown emitter powers up and aims at Kestrel Seven.

It transmits one pure, unmodulated tone, a C-W, right inside Task Force Orion's carrier.

The satellite is a bent pipe. Whatever reaches its uplink goes out on every downlink, tone included.

On the ground, the net falls apart. Signal quality collapses from ten decibels to barely two and a half. Chat won't send. Voice breaks up.

At the regional ops center, the alarm goes up: Task Force Orion's command net is degraded. Nobody knows yet what's causing it.

On a spectrum analyzer, a healthy carrier looks like a flat, noisy plateau.

With the jammer on, a thin spike pokes out of it, narrower than the analyzer can resolve in terms of bandwidth. That's your target.

You are the SATCOM watch on Kestrel Seven. Your tool is the spectrum analyzer.

Find it. Characterize it. Report it. Restore the link.
